01

Humble Beginnings

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was born on 15 October 1931 in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu. Growing up in a modest family, he developed an early appreciation for learning and hard work. His journey reminds us that our beginnings do not have to define our future.

02

The Scientist

After studying physics and aerospace engineering, Kalam dedicated decades of his career to India's scientific progress. At ISRO, he led the SLV-III project, which successfully placed the Rohini satellite into near-Earth orbit in 1980. His work also contributed significantly to India's missile development programmes.

03

The People's President

On 25 July 2002, Kalam became the eleventh President of India. He was admired for his humility, approachability and commitment to national development. Rather than remaining distant from ordinary people, he consistently reached out to students and young citizens.

04

The Teacher Who Never Stopped Teaching

After his presidency, Kalam continued to teach, write and speak about India's future. Books such as Wings of Fire and Ignited Minds carried his ideas to readers across generations. He died on 27 July 2015 while delivering a lecture at IIM Shillong, leaving behind a legacy rooted in education, service and hope.
